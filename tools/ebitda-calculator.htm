What is EBITDA?

EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. It is widely considered one of the most important metrics in corporate finance. It measures a company's raw, operational profitability by stripping away accounting decisions, tax environments, and debt financing.

Why Do Investors Add Those Things Back?

If you are buying a business, you don't care how the previous owner financed their debt (Interest) or how their specific accountant handled asset write-offs (Depreciation). You want to know exactly how much hard cash the core business operations generate. Adding those non-operational expenses back to the Net Income gives buyers a clear picture of the company's true cash-generating power.

Understanding Enterprise Value & Multiples

When private equity firms or competitors buy a company, they rarely just look at Revenue. They buy the company for a Multiple of EBITDA. For example, a standard brick-and-mortar business might sell for 3x or 4x its EBITDA. A high-growth software company (SaaS) might sell for 10x or 15x its EBITDA.
